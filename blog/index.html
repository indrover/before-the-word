Panini's Ashtadhyayi: The First Generative Model Soon
In 350 BCE, a grammarian in northwest India encoded all valid Sanskrit into 3,959 rules — ordered, recursive, and finite. What he built looks, structurally, like what we now call a large language model. The parallel is not metaphorical.

The Four Levels of Vāk: What LLMs Process and What They Miss Soon
Ancient Indian philosophy described four levels of speech: Parā, Paśyantī, Madhyamā, Vaikharī — from the unmanifest vibration of meaning to the spoken word. Modern language models operate fluently at one of them.

Sphoṭa and the Embedding Space Soon
Bhartrhari proposed that meaning is not assembled word by word but grasped whole — a single unitary flash he called sphoṭa. The embedding space of a language model encodes something similar, and reveals where the analogy breaks.
